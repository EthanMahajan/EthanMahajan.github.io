## Ideation

I chose a chess set because I enjoy chess and wanted to learn the Invention Studio's fabrication tools. I wanted the board and pieces to work as a coordinated set, with a clean appearance and stable pieces that remain practical to manufacture.

The set lets me learn engraving for the playing surface and machining for the planned metal pieces. I separated these stages so I could work on the board before committing to the more involved piece fabrication.

<h2 id="design">Planned metal pieces and set design</h2>

I designed the board's playing surface in CAD for laser engraving. The planned pieces have a low center of mass to improve stability during play. I still need to develop and test the CNC machining approach.

As I develop the set, I need to evaluate how the engraving defines the playing surface, how the board is finished, and how the pieces feel and stand during use.

## Board fabrication

I started with a 5 × 5 ft sheet of 3/4-inch birch plywood and rough-cut it into 16 board blanks. At Georgia Tech’s Invention Studio, a student makerspace, I used the table saw to cut a blank square, then sanded it before taking it to the laser cutter for engraving.

I later added a walnut border. I ripped a walnut board into narrower stock, then used a miter saw to cut the strips for the edges. After gluing them to the board, I engraved the rank and file labels along the border.

The video shows the laser engraving the playing surface. I also personalized the reverse with my name and a QR code.
